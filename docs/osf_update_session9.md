## Why this update

Fiorillo is an open medical model I am building. It answers typed questions (one answer from a fixed set) with calibrated probabilities. This registration fixed the test of its version 0.4 on EI before any test prediction. EI asks: given a randomized trial's article and a prompt that names an intervention, a comparator and an outcome, did the outcome significantly increase, significantly decrease, or show no significant difference?

v0.4's result. v0.4 met all four criteria. Its EI specialist, s8_ei, averages three fine-tuning runs of Qwen3-4B-Base with LoRA adapters and Laya's decision head. On EI's test split (1,218 prompts in 333 articles), its macro-F1 was 0.9399 (0.9225 to 0.9544), its accuracy 0.9409 (0.9246 to 0.9554) and its log loss 0.1994 (0.1573 to 0.2486). Its ECE was 0.0119, against a limit of 0.0500 (H1). Its log loss minus the prior's was -0.8773 (-0.9225 to -0.8281) (H2), and minus Gemma 4 31B-it's -0.2084 (-0.2720 to -0.1505) (H3), each with 10,000 article resamples. Its macro-F1 was not below Gemma 4 31B-it's, 0.8626 (H4). These figures are from my v0.4 report (docs/fiorillo_v0_4.md, Tables 5 and 8). The macro-F1 is above every published EI result I found. It is about the rate at which EI's own verifiers judged the prompt writers' answers correct (94.0 percent; the annotators' 90.0 percent; DeYoung et al. 2020, section 2.3).

v0.4's licenses. EI's articles come from PMC's open-access subset, and each keeps its own license. On 1 October 2026 I read each EI article's own license statement as PMC serves it (the license recheck: src/tools/ei_license_recheck.py, with its results in results/fiorillo_v0_5/license_recheck.csv and license_recheck.json). By those statements, 1,226 of the 2,657 training articles that s8_ei read are under non-commercial, no-derivatives, share-alike, verbatim-only or publishers' own terms, or state no license. Whether weights trained on them may be published is UNVERIFIABLE: no source I found addresses it. So s8_ei stays a private research model. The version I would release is v0.5. Every EI article its EI specialist learns from is CC BY, CC0 or the public domain by its own statement. The specialist starts from s7_b3, which learned from PubMedQA's abstracts (see the last section).

The checks v0.4 needs. No check yet bounds how much of s8_ei's result comes from what Qwen3-4B-Base read before this project. EI's labels have been public since 2019, and the trials' results are in PubMed. After the v0.4 results I added descriptive checks, which are deviations from this registration (session 8's corrections 14 and 15). They found both models less accurate where no annotated evidence span lies inside the input. That is consistent with reading the evidence. It is also consistent with those prompts being harder for any reader, and it does not rule out that a model recalls some answers. Those checks cannot show whether the pretraining text held EI's annotations. So session 9 asks s8_ei the test prompts with no article, with the title alone, and with the intervention and the comparator exchanged. These are the reading checks.

What session 9 does. It runs the reading checks on s8_ei before any training. It builds v0.5, a routed system. PubMedQA's question goes to s7_b3 with its session 7 calibration, unchanged. EI's question goes to a new EI specialist, trained only on clean EI articles with a recipe chosen on validation. The readmission question goes to the readmission pool that the rule below chooses. v0.5 hands nothing off to a larger model. The session judges v0.5 by v0.4's four criteria on EI's test split, with v0.5's EI specialist in s8_ei's place. If the bar is met, v0.5 is released publicly, and I end the embargo of https://osf.io/kaxmn/, and of this update if it is filed as a separate registration, the same day.

This update names every analysis that session 9 runs on EI's test split, in order, and every rule fixed before them. I file it before anything in session 9 reads EI's test labels or a count of them, except the records builder (the replay of its rebuild included), which wrote and counted them, and the prints of its counts that I describe below.

### The reading checks' inputs and scores

The inputs are made from the test records. All 1,218 prompts appear in none. Title and swap leave out the prompts named below, and these are counted.
- none: the article is set to the empty text.
- title: the article is set to its title. The title is the text after the label "TITLE:" on its line, or else from the first line after it that is not blank, up to the first blank line or the first line that begins, after its leading spaces, with "ABSTRACT" or "BODY", with its whitespace collapsed (corrections 6 and 7 (e)). A prompt whose article gives no title is left out: 9 test prompts, so title holds 1,209.
- swap: the intervention and the comparator are exchanged in the prompt. The article is as in the system's own input: 4,096 tokens for s8_ei, and the winning recipe's length for v0.5's specialist. The expected answer is exchanged for "significantly increased" and "significantly decreased", and kept for "no significant difference". A prompt whose intervention and comparator are the same text, after whitespace and case are collapsed, is left out. No test prompt is, so swap holds 1,218 at either length.

The systems are s8_ei (its three runs' raw probabilities averaged, then session 8's calibration), before any training, and v0.5's EI specialist with its own calibration, after the freeze. If swap averaging is kept, v0.5's none and title checks use its single pass, without swap averaging, and the results say so.

For each system and input, each with a 95 percent interval from 1,000 article resamples:
- accuracy and macro-F1, against the original answers (none and title) or the exchanged ones (swap);
- for none and title, the share of answers equal to the system's answer on its full input;
- for swap, the share of "significantly increased" and "significantly decreased" answers that exchange, and of "no significant difference" answers that stay; and the same for the prompts whose comparator names a time point or a baseline (its text matches baseline, before, pre-, pretreatment, prior to or start, ignoring case), since for those the exchanged statement reads less naturally, though it is still correct.

The readings, and the stop, are under the rules below.

### The hand-off analyses

These are secondary and descriptive. v0.5 hands nothing off, and none of this is part of v0.5.
- Pairs: EI with s8_ei and Gemma 4 31B-it; EI with v0.5's EI specialist and Gemma 4 31B-it (on the longer input if criterion 3 uses it); PubMedQA with s7_b3 and Gemma 4 31B-it. Each pair runs on validation before the freeze (for EI, all 1,237 validation prompts, with v0.5's calibration applied to all of them) and on test after it.
- The risk-coverage curve: the specialist's error rate among the answers it keeps, at coverages from 5 to 100 percent in steps of 5. AURC is computed per item: the mean, over every coverage k/n for k from 1 to n, of the error rate of the k most confident answers. E-AURC is AURC minus the AURC of a perfect ranking of the same answers. AUGRC is as Traub et al. 2024 define it.
- Deferral curves: the combined accuracy at hand-off rates from 0 to 100 percent in steps of 5, for the confidence rule as session 8 ranks it; for random hand-off (its expected value); for the oracle (the items the specialist gets wrong and the large model gets right are handed off first, which bounds what any rule could gain); and for hand-off ranked by the large model's own confidence (a reference, not a usable rule, since it needs the large model's answer on every item).
- Intervals at the registered rates (10, 20, 30 and 50 percent) and for the areas, from 1,000 resamples (EI by article, PubMedQA by item).
- A learned rule, EI only: a logistic regression (L2, C 1.0), fitted on all validation prompts, predicts "the specialist wrong and the large model right" from the specialist's calibrated top probability, the margin between its top two answers, its entropy, and the standard deviation across its runs of the probability each run gives the averaged top answer (for s9_ei_wavg, its source runs). With two runs (correction 7 (b)'s path), the run spread is the sample standard deviation over those two, and a frozen rule is applied only with as many runs as it was fitted on; it stops otherwise (correction 8 (a)). With swap averaging kept, the run spread, and the top answer it is read at, come from each run's single-pass probabilities, because no run has a swap-averaged file of its own; the specialist's own answers are swap-averaged (correction 8 (f)). At each registered rate, the items with the highest predicted probability are handed off. The rule is fitted and frozen on validation (for s8_ei in step 6 of the plan, for v0.5's specialist in the calibrate stage) and compared on test with the confidence rule at the same rate (paired, 1,000 article resamples). It is never released. On validation, s8_ei was wrong where Gemma 4 31B-it was right on 31 of 1,237 prompts (2.5 percent), and the confidence rule's 10 percent hand-off held 23 of them and 23 prompts the other way (derived). So a better rule has room of at most about 2.5 points of accuracy, and few cases to learn from; this is stated with the result.

### The license-clean records

v0.5's EI specialist learns only from clean EI articles. Nothing it learns (its weights, its calibration, the recipe's choice) may come from an EI article that is not clean, with one exception, below.
- Clean. An article is clean when license_recheck.csv's clean column is true. Its own statement, as PMC serves it, is CC BY (any version, by a link to the license or in words), CC0 or the public domain, and no part of it (a license link, a license type, the license text or a copyright statement) restricts commercial use, derivatives or the license of what is made from it (non-commercial, no derivatives, share alike, verbatim or unmodified, in any wording that src/tools/ei_license_recheck.py lists). Where the parts disagree, the more restrictive part decides. A statement that names a publisher's own terms (iOpenAccess) is not clean, even beside Creative Commons words. CC BY-SA (2 training articles) is left out as well, because Creative Commons' conservative guidance is that a model trained on ShareAlike works should carry the same license.
- Why the articles' own statements. Europe PMC's records, which my v0.4 report used, were wrong in both directions: 47 training articles that Europe PMC lists as CC BY state other terms or none in their own records, and 12 that it lists with no license state CC BY.
- Correction 7 (a). A test of the recheck's tool found three rules that misread made-up statements (a CC0 waiver that covers only the data, a government work, and the general rule on federal employees' contributions). The three rules were fixed. Run again on all 3,348 articles, the fixed tool gives every article the class that license_recheck.csv records, so the clean set did not change.
- The clean training records: session 8's training records (EI at commit a661e8c14f973398380c8865cf2f27a535aaaf6d) whose article is clean, in the same order, byte for byte: 5,279 of the 10,046 training prompts, in 1,431 of the 2,657 training articles.
- The clean validation records: 654 of the 1,237 validation prompts, in 179 of the 340 validation articles. They are the only validation prompts that any choice for v0.5 reads.
- The monitoring subset: 200 clean validation prompts (in 113 articles), drawn with random.Random(20260927).sample over the clean validation records sorted by ID as text. Only the training runs' logged validation reads it, never anything that chooses.
- The test records: session 8's, all 1,218 prompts, unchanged.
- The exception: the passage selection's word statistics (data/processed/session8/bm25_stats.json: for each word, how many passages of the 2,657 training articles contain it, with the number of passages and their mean length). They hold single words and counts, but no sentence of any article. They are kept so that every input (s8_ei's, Gemma 4 31B-it's and v0.5's) is cut by the same rule. They ship with the release, so that a user can cut a new article the same way, and the model card states the exception.

### The recipes and the recipe rule

Every run uses src/train/train_qwen_head.py train and predict as they are, from s7_b3's folder (--init, --keep last, --freeze-steps 0): it starts from s7_b3's adapters, projection and head, the last weights are kept, and nothing selects a checkpoint. Shared settings: Qwen3-4B-Base at revision 906bfd4b4dc7f14ee4320094d8b41684abff8539; LoRA rank 16, alpha 32, dropout 0.05; AdamW, with 1e-4 for the projection and the head; a cosine schedule to 1e-6; gradient clipping at 1.0; weight decay 0.01; bfloat16 autocast; 32 records per optimizer step; logged validation on the monitoring subset (--eval-every 80); and every other setting as s8_ei's in session 8. Runs at 4,096 tokens use session 8's memory setting (micro-batch 1 with accumulation 32). After training, every run predicts the whole validation split (1,237 prompts); only the 654 clean prompts are scored.

The recipes:
- base (b): session 8's recipe on the clean training records: 2 epochs, inputs of 4,096 tokens, the adapters' learning rate 5e-5. Three runs, s9_b_1, s9_b_2 and s9_b_3, with seeds 20260927, 20260928 and 20260929. Their probability average is s9_b.
- swap: the clean training records plus a swapped copy of each (ID <id>-swap): the intervention and the comparator exchanged in the prompt; "significantly increased" and "significantly decreased" exchanged in the label and the target; "no significant difference" kept. A prompt whose intervention and comparator are the same text, after whitespace and case are collapsed, gets no copy. One clean training prompt has the same text in both, so the file holds 10,557 records (5,278 copies). One epoch over the doubled set, so a run takes about as many optimizer steps as base.
- long: inputs selected by session 8's rule at 6,144 tokens instead of 4,096 (with 16 to spare; BM25's statistics unchanged), for training, monitoring, validation and test. On the whole training split, these inputs keep the marked evidence for 99.03 percent of prompts (98.68 percent on the clean training records). That is within 1 point of the plan's check (98.91 percent), so no correction was needed. A pilot chooses the memory setting: a setting passes if it does not run out of memory and its peak is at most 76 GiB, and the long runs use the passing setting with the higher training rate. If none passes, long is NOT BUILT and dropped from the candidates.
- lr1: the adapters' learning rate 1e-4.
- lr2: the adapters' learning rate 2e-4.
- Two runs for each candidate (correction 2): swap, long, lr1 and lr2 are each trained twice, with seeds 20260927 and 20260928 (s9_<candidate>_1 and s9_<candidate>_2). The first runs of all four come before any second run.

The rule. The train stage applies it on the rented GPU by itself, without waiting for me. Nothing in it reads a test file.
- A run's score: from its raw validation predictions on the 654 clean prompts, the out-of-fold log loss of session 8's calibration rule (the lower of the temperature and temperature_intercepts families' out-of-fold log losses; 5 folds, 20 repeats, seed 20260927). An average's score is the same, computed on the averaged probabilities.
- The margin m: the largest minus the smallest of the scores of s9_b_1, s9_b_2 and s9_b_3, and at least 0.005.
- A candidate's score is the mean of its two runs' scores. Base's score for the comparison is the mean of s9_b_1's and s9_b_2's. A candidate passes if its score is below base's by more than m and each of its two runs scores below the base run with the same seed.
- lr1 and lr2 count as one change, the learning rate. If both pass, the one with the lower mean score is kept.
- The combination: if two or more of the three changes (swap, long and the learning rate) pass, one more recipe uses every passing change at once, in two runs, s9_combo_1 and s9_combo_2 (seeds 20260927 and 20260928). It passes if its score (the mean of its two runs) is below the best passing candidate's by more than m, and each of its runs scores below that candidate's run with the same seed.
- The winning recipe: the combination if it passed; otherwise the passing candidate with the lowest score; otherwise base.
- If the winning recipe is not base, it is trained once more, with seed 20260929 (s9_<recipe>_3). s9_ei is the probability average of the winning recipe's three runs (for base, s9_b).
- The weight average, s9_ei_wavg: one model built from the same three runs. Each layer's three LoRA updates are averaged exactly, by joining the three runs' A matrices along the rank and dividing their B matrices by 3 (rank 48, alpha 96, so that alpha divided by the rank stays 2). The projection's and the head's weights are averaged elementwise. It predicts the whole validation split. v0.5 uses s9_ei_wavg in place of s9_ei if its score is not above s9_ei's by more than m (it costs one forward pass a question instead of three). If the cost rule leaves the winning recipe with two runs (its first and third), s9_ei_wavg is built from those two (rank 32, alpha 64, B divided by 2) and compared with the same two runs' probability average by the same test (correction 7 (b)). On that path, the learned hand-off rule's fit and apply and the test-prediction stage take the two runs (correction 8 (a)).
- Swap averaging (correction 3). For each prompt, the specialist answers the prompt as written and the prompt with its intervention and comparator exchanged (the article as in its own input). In the second answer, the probabilities of "significantly increased" and "significantly decreased" are exchanged, and the two answers are averaged. The averaging is of raw probabilities, before calibration; for s9_ei, each run's two answers are averaged and the runs then averaged. A prompt whose intervention and comparator are the same text keeps its single answer. After the weight average's decision, the system v0.5 would use (s9_ei or s9_ei_wavg) predicts the exchanged validation prompts (all 1,237, at its recipe's length). v0.5 uses swap averaging if its swap-averaged score on the 654 clean prompts is below its plain score by more than m. On test, the exchanged inputs are the reading checks' swap inputs, and nothing more is predicted.
- One run (correction 8 (b)). A candidate that the cost rule leaves with one run passes if that run's score is below s9_b_1's by more than m (the rule as first written), and this is recorded. A combination left with one run, or one whose best candidate has one run, passes if its first run's score is below the best candidate's first run's by more than m.
- recipe_selection.json records every score, m, every pass or fail with its numbers, the combination's parts, the winning recipe, the weight average's decision, the swap-averaging decision and the time of each decision.

### Calibration

- v0.5's EI specialist (s9_ei or s9_ei_wavg, with or without swap averaging, as the rules above decide) is calibrated from the logarithms of its averaged (or single) probabilities on the 654 clean validation prompts. Its family is the one with the lowest out-of-fold log loss (5 folds, 20 repeats, seed 20260927) among four:
  - temperature: softmax(log p / T);
  - temperature_intercepts: softmax(log p / T + b), with the first answer's intercept fixed at 0;
  - vector: a scale and an intercept for each answer (5 free parameters, since adding the same constant to every intercept changes nothing);
  - dirichlet_odir: a 3 by 3 matrix on the log-probabilities and an intercept for each answer, with L2 penalties on the off-diagonal entries and on the intercepts, each strength chosen from 0.0001, 0.001, 0.01, 0.1 and 1 (25 pairs) by 5-fold cross-validation inside each fitting set, its folds drawn with the seed.
- An exact tie (within 1e-12) goes to the family listed first. The chosen family is then fitted on all 654. The choice is made after the swap-averaging decision, on the system v0.5 uses.
- For K8 and K10, what v0.5 does not use (the other of s9_ei and s9_ei_wavg, and the other form of swap averaging) is calibrated by the same family, fitted on its own clean validation predictions.
- Every other system keeps session 8's rule: temperature or temperature_intercepts, whichever has the lower out-of-fold log loss (an exact tie goes to temperature). s9_b and each run are calibrated on the 654 clean validation prompts. s8_ei and Gemma 4 31B-it keep session 8's calibrations, and s7_b3 its session 7 calibration. gemma4_31b_long is calibrated on all 1,237 EI validation prompts, and BioGPT-Large-PubMedQA on PubMedQA's 150 calibrate items (secondary, as K5 says).
- The ECE limit for criterion 1: the larger of 0.05 and the 95th percentile of the ECE that a perfectly calibrated model shows by chance at the test size, 1,218 prompts. src/eval/ece_noise_floor.py, unchanged, draws 5,000 simulated test sets (seed 20260927), as this registration describes for H1, from v0.5's EI specialist's calibrated clean validation predictions. The limit is written to ece_limit_evidence_effect.json before any test prediction of a v0.5 system exists.
- calibration_s9.json records each system's family and parameters, and the four families' out-of-fold table for v0.5's EI specialist, whatever is chosen.

### The readings of the reading checks, and the stop

The readings are fixed now and reported with the results. They decide how I word the EI result. They gate no release.
- none. Macro-F1 at or below 0.60: "its answers depend on the article's text". Above 0.75: "the prompt alone carries more than a classifier trained on prompts alone (0.511 macro-F1, DeYoung et al. 2020, Table 1); it may come from EI's training prompts (a model of 4 billion parameters may learn stronger priors from them than that classifier did), from pretraining, or from EI's public labels, and these checks cannot tell them apart". Otherwise: "partly".
- title. The paired difference in macro-F1, title minus none, on the 1,209 prompts in both inputs, with the same resamples, and its interval (correction 8 (g)). Wholly above 0.05: "the title adds information about the result: a title can state the result, or the model may recall the trial, and these checks cannot tell them apart". Wholly below 0.05: "no added knowledge of 0.05 or more was detected". Otherwise: "not settled".
- swap. Accuracy against the exchanged answers at or above 0.85: "it follows the comparison as written". Below 0.70: "it does not follow the exchange (recall of the trial or a bias toward one direction)". Otherwise: "partly".
- v0.5's swap check. If v0.5's recipe includes swap augmentation, its swap reading is reported with the caveat that it measures what the augmentation taught as much as reading. If swap averaging is kept, v0.5's answer to an exchanged prompt is the exchange of its answer to the prompt, so its swap check is consistent by construction. It is then reported as such and not read as a reading check. s8_ei's swap check is the one that bears on reading.
- The stop. If s8_ei's macro-F1 with no article is above 0.85, the session stops before any training and asks me, with the checks' numbers. The rented GPU waits, at about $3.52 an hour, counted toward the cap, and I am reminded every hour. If I have not answered within 3 hours, what exists is packaged, copied and checked, and the GPU is shut down.
- References, stated beside the results: the training split's answer shares score a macro-F1 of 0.1976 on the test split (my v0.4 report, Table 5); and frontier models scored an accuracy of 0.79 to 0.88 from trial titles alone on 250 oncology trials, on a two-answer task (positive or negative), against a majority class of 0.584 (Windisch et al. 2026, JMIR AI; checked at its source in step 0).
