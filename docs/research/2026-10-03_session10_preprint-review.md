How I reviewed. I read the plan's THE PREPRINT, THE CHECKS, DECISIONS and corrections 1 to 4 first, then the draft page by page in the PDF (tables and figures as rendered), then the two results reports, the registration, the update and the drafter's registration check. I checked every number in the text and in Tables 1 to 12 against the reports and against the results files' keys (comparison_v0_5.json: metrics, comparisons, bar_intervals, release_bar, ece_limits, k3, k4.table, k4.references, k6.choice, k7.pairs, k9, k10, sensitivity, calibration_slope_intercept, cost_per_1000_questions, not_built, comparisons_not_made; comparison_v0_4.json: g3). A script also matched all 856 decimal numbers in the extracted text against every value in the two files at the printed precision; I traced by hand each number it could not match. I recomputed the top reliability bin's Wilson interval, counted the abstract's words, and searched the source for the plan's banned words, dashes, British spellings, first person plural and missing thousands separators. Numbers that session 10 produced itself (s9_long_3's figures, the reliability bins) are not in my inputs; they are listed at the end as not checked.

Result: 15 findings marked must fix and 16 marked should fix. Page numbers are the PDF's.

## Must fix

- Where: page 1, Abstract.
- What is wrong: the abstract has 263 words before the usage check's sentence is added (whitespace-separated words of the .tex abstract with the placeholder removed; 265 counted from the PDF's text; 232 even if numbers are not counted as words). The usage check's sentence and the clause finding 4 asks for will add more.
- Evidence: prompt_session10.md, THE PREPRINT: "Abstract: at most 250 words". Words per sentence: 19, 38, 30, 15, 59, 4, 32, 51, 15.
- Suggested fix: cut about 40 words (13 to reach the cap, plus room for the usage check's sentence and finding 4's clause). Candidates: "(4 billion parameters)" (the title says small, Section 4 gives the size); fold "All four criteria passed." into the criteria sentence; shorten the second sentence (see finding 2). Keep the abstract under arXiv's 1,920-character limit for the abstract field as well: it is 1,650 characters now, before the usage check's sentence (derived).

**2. The abstract says the specialist "reads the full text" of the article.**
- Where: page 1, Abstract, second sentence: "Its main specialist reads the full text of a randomized trial's article".
- What is wrong: the specialist reads the prompt, the title and abstract, and the passages chosen by BM25 that fit in 6,144 tokens, not the full text. The sentence overstates what was tested and contradicts Sections 4 and 10.
- Evidence: draft, Section 4 (page 4): "Each input holds the intervention, the comparator and the outcome, then the article's title and abstract, then passages chosen by BM25 against the prompt's words, results-section passages first, while they fit"; Section 10 (page 11): "with the article cut to 6,144 tokens"; fiorillo_v0_5.md, The recipes: "long: inputs selected by session 8's rule at 6,144 tokens ...; the marked evidence is kept for 98.68 percent of the clean training prompts."
- Suggested fix: "Its main specialist reads a randomized trial's article (its title, abstract and the passages that fit in 6,144 tokens) and answers whether ...".

**3. "Trained only on" openly licensed articles overstates what the released specialist learned from.**
- Where: page 1, Abstract ("trained only on the 1,431 of EI's 2,657 training articles whose own license statement allows reuse"); page 12, Section 11, first sentence ("A 4-billion-parameter open model, trained only on articles whose licenses allow reuse, met a bar fixed in advance"). Milder instances: page 2, Section 1, second bullet; page 3, Section 3, end of The license recheck ("the released specialist learned only from clean articles"); page 10, Section 8, second paragraph; page 12, Section 10, fifth bullet ("with one exception", followed by a second exception).
- What is wrong: the released specialist started from the PubMedQA specialist's weights, which learned from PubMedQA's abstracts whose own licenses were not checked one by one, and its passage selection uses word statistics counted over all 2,657 training articles. The Discussion's sentence is therefore not true as written, and the abstract reads the same way. The body discloses both facts; the summary sentences do not.
- Evidence: draft, Section 4: "I trained it from the weights of the project's earlier PubMedQA specialist"; Section 3, PubMedQA: "unlike EI's articles, those abstracts' own licenses were not checked one by one, and most PubMed abstracts are the publishers'"; osf_update_session9.md, PubMedQA in v0.5: "v0.5's EI runs start from s7_b3's folder, and s7_b3 learned from PubMedQA's abstracts ... Abstract: "fine-tuned for EI only on the 1,431 ...". Section 10: "with two exceptions". Elsewhere: "only on clean EI articles".
